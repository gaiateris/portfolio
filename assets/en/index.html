Case study

Yoyomove: visual identity and design system

I designed the Yoyomove logo, the Yo symbol and the brand’s design system: the rules that keep the identity consistent across web, social, email, events and print.

My role
Logo, Yo symbol and design system
Brand
Yoyomove, long-term car rental
Markets
Italy, Spain, France, Portugal, the Netherlands, Belgium
Design system
Version 1.0, available online
  • Creative

See the live design system (in Italian) (opens in a new tab)

01 · Concept

A light brand, like a yo-yo

The name expresses dynamism and lightness: the guiding idea is mobility that is “flexible and simple, like a yo-yo”. The payoff “Move better, everyday” closes the brand manifesto.

The four design principles

  • Simple above all
  • Transparent by default
  • Human, not institutional
  • Dynamic like a yo-yo

02 · Logo and symbol

The Yoyomove logo and the Yo symbol

Soft, rounded shapes convey lightness and movement. In the Yo symbol the “o” recalls a yo-yo, with the “y” as its string, and holds a steering wheel. The symbol works on its own where the full logo doesn’t fit: avatars, favicons, apps, merchandise.

Usage rules

  • Positive, negative and monochrome versions
  • Clear space equal to the height of the symbol, on all sides
  • Below 120 px, in headers, emails and banners, the symbol replaces the logo
  • On yellow backgrounds, monochrome version only

03 · Design system

From guidelines to components

The design system has three parts: brand guidelines (introduction, brand, tone of voice), visual identity (logo, colour, typography, graphic elements) and digital (design tokens, UI components, downloads).

What it defines

  • Primary palette: Yoyomove Yellow #FFCC00 and Yoyomove Black #000000
  • Sofia Pro for headings and interface, Rogliano for body text
  • Graphic elements: the yellow wave, outline-only circles, the cord, the Yo symbol as a signature
  • Contrast rules to stay within WCAG 2.1 AA
  • CSS design tokens and UI components on Bootstrap 5.3

04 · Applications

The identity at work

The same system across marketing materials: the Yo symbol as a pattern and as a lapel pin in social visuals, the logo at the top of the newsletter, the brand yellow in event posters.
